Why Technical Translation Needs More Than Machine Translation

A human-reviewed workflow for translating engineering documents, BOMs, specifications, quality reports, and export communication.

By Rohan YangJune 18, 20266 min read

Why Technical Translation Is Different From General Business Translation

Technical translation for manufacturing companies is not simply a language task. Engineering documents define what is being made, how it should be produced, and how acceptance will be judged. A drawing note, material specification, tolerance, inspection requirement, or process description can influence a quotation, a production decision, or a customer's approval. A sentence can sound fluent in English and still be technically wrong.

Manufacturing information is also highly connected. A term in a drawing may appear again in a bill of materials (BOM), process flow, inspection plan, quality report, and customer email. If the same component or operation is translated differently across documents, teams can misunderstand whether the documents refer to the same item. Consistency is therefore as important as sentence-level accuracy.

Companies working with overseas customers need a repeatable technical translation workflow that preserves units, values, references, revision status, and engineering meaning. AI can speed up parts of the process, but it should operate inside a controlled review system rather than replace technical ownership.

Which Manufacturing Documents Need Specialist Translation

Common document types include engineering drawings, BOMs, product specifications, material certificates, work instructions, process flow charts, control plans, inspection reports, non-conformance reports, corrective-action documents, packaging instructions, and supplier quality requirements. Each has its own conventions and risks. A customer email may allow more flexibility in phrasing; a drawing note or acceptance criterion usually does not.

Engineering drawing translation requires special care around datum references, geometric dimensioning and tolerancing (GD&T), surface finish, weld symbols, thread specifications, and revision notes. BOM translation requires stable part names, material descriptions, quantities, and component references. Quality documents need consistent terms for inspection, sampling, traceability, defect classification, and corrective action.

Before translating, identify the document's purpose, audience, source revision, and approval status. Do not treat an outdated drawing as the current production instruction. If the source is ambiguous or incomplete, flag the issue for the responsible engineer instead of guessing what the author intended.

Where AI Translation Helps—and Where It Can Fail

AI translation is useful for producing a first draft, translating repetitive email exchanges, aligning bilingual tables, summarizing long customer specifications, and identifying terminology that should be added to a company glossary. It can also help teams compare two versions of a document and list phrases that appear to have changed, provided the source files are handled securely and the comparison is reviewed.

Machine-only translation can fail when a term has a specialized manufacturing meaning, when abbreviations are ambiguous, or when tables and drawing references are separated from their context. It may normalize a number format, omit a negative sign, alter a unit, translate a proper product name, or make a requirement sound optional when it is mandatory. These errors are not always obvious from a fluent final sentence.

Never ask a model to infer a missing tolerance, material grade, test method, or acceptance limit. If the source does not provide the information, the translation should preserve that absence and raise a query. AI should improve speed and consistency, not silently make engineering decisions.

Build a Manufacturing Translation Glossary

A bilingual glossary is one of the highest-value assets a manufacturer can create. It should record the approved source term, target-language equivalent, definition or context, related process, and any prohibited alternatives. Include product and component names, machine operations, tooling terminology, quality terms, material grades, abbreviations, and customer-specific vocabulary where permitted.

Do not create a glossary from machine suggestions alone. Ask engineering, quality, and sales to approve terms that affect product definition or acceptance. Where a term has more than one valid translation, record when each version should be used. For example, a word may refer to a physical tool in one context and a process operation in another.

Store the glossary in a shared, version-controlled location and use it across email templates, quotations, drawings, BOMs, and work instructions. When a customer has a mandated term or naming convention, record it separately and follow the applicable contract or supplier requirement.

A Human-in-the-Loop Workflow for Engineering Document Translation

First, classify the document by risk. Routine correspondence may need a commercial review; drawings, specifications, control plans, and acceptance criteria need qualified technical review. Next, confirm the source file, revision, language pair, intended audience, and any customer terminology requirements. Remove or protect confidential information in accordance with company policy before using an external AI service.

Second, prepare the translation with context. Provide the model with the document type, manufacturing process, target audience, approved glossary, and instructions to preserve numbers, units, part identifiers, tables, and formatting. Ask it to flag ambiguous terms rather than resolve them. Keep source and translated content side by side wherever possible.

Third, run structured checks. Compare all numbers, units, tolerances, dates, revision identifiers, part numbers, and cross-references. Check that tables have the same row and column structure. Have an engineer review technical meaning and a language-qualified reviewer check readability. Record approval and revision history before the translated document is released.

Control Revisions, Confidentiality, and Accountability

Translation errors often occur because teams work from different revisions. Put the source document number, revision, translation revision, date, and approval status on controlled files. When the source changes, identify which translated documents must be updated. Do not overwrite an approved translation without retaining the prior revision and its review record.

Manufacturing documents may contain customer drawings, product designs, pricing, supplier details, or proprietary process information. Check the organization's data-handling policy before uploading material to any AI platform or third-party translation service. Use approved enterprise controls where required, restrict access, and avoid including unnecessary customer-identifying information in prompts.

Finally, define who is accountable. AI can draft, compare, and organize; the company remains responsible for the accuracy of information sent to customers or used in production. Clear ownership prevents a convenient automation from becoming an uncontrolled source of engineering risk.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can AI translate engineering drawings accurately?

AI can assist with drawing notes and supporting text, but it should not be trusted to interpret all symbols, dimensions, GD&T, or engineering intent without specialist review. Preserve the drawing itself and have a qualified engineer verify critical requirements.

What is the best way to translate a manufacturing BOM?

Use a controlled bilingual part-name glossary, preserve item numbers and quantities, retain the table structure, and review material and component descriptions with engineering or purchasing. Do not allow automated translation to change identifiers.

How do manufacturers maintain consistent technical terminology?

Create an approved bilingual glossary, assign technical owners, record context and prohibited alternatives, and reuse the terms across drawings, quotations, work instructions, and customer communication.

Should confidential customer drawings be uploaded to public AI tools?

Only if the company's customer agreements and data policies explicitly permit it and the service's data controls are appropriate. Otherwise use approved internal tools or a controlled translation process.

Practical Checklist

  • Classify the document by technical and commercial risk.
  • Confirm source revision, purpose, audience, and approved terminology.
  • Use AI for drafting and organization, not for inventing missing specifications.
  • Compare values and references, obtain human approval, and retain revision history.
